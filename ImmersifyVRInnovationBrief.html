CITYPLUG ANSWER: Our project uses artificial intelligence to determine optimal locations for EV chargers. In this, we make use of two algorithms to analyze geospatial data and extract insights invisible to human eyes. Our ‘City Model,’ makes use of clustering, a means by which geographic data is broken up into regions based on their attributes and suitability for EV’s. Each cluster represents a series of points with similar land use, infrastructure, and other important attributes. Clusters offer an easy way to plan charging strategy by region, allowing easy identification of areas suitable for EV expansion and areas which lack suitability. Within each cluster, all points with the potential to host public chargers - this excludes private residences and similar land uses - are analyzed based on their own attributes and scores. The score is determined by the factors which contribute to a building’s charging demand and suitability, such as its distance to the nearest highway exit, the land use of the building (i.e. whether it is a commercial center or restaurant), and the electricity available at that site. The factors that influence the score, as well as their relative weights, can be set from customer to customer, allowing them to prioritize the attributes of regions that best suit the particular demands of their charging rollout strategy or business plan. While this algorithm handles charging within cities, our second algorithm, the ‘Long Range Model,’ handles charging in between major urban centers. The long range model analyzes traffic at freeway exits, and analyzes suitable sites adjacent to those exits in much the same manner that the City Model analyzes points within cities, considering factors that impact the site’s suitability. In the long range model exclusively, however, the traffic at an intersection is given the most importance in a site’s selection, as this traffic will comprise nearly the entirety of the site’s demand. Such an innovation has the potential to create profound and lasting changes for humanity. Our team has observed, both in our home state California and abroad, voracious natural disasters which ruin lives and leave people homeless, injured, or dead. These disasters of increasing intensity are being fueled by climate change, the most pressing problem of our day. Hence, it is of critical importance to rise and meet mankind's biggest challenge with the full arsenal of tools available to us. The emissions of the transportation sector are incredibly important to reduce, accounting for 27% of all greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions in the U.S., the largest single contributor, and 49% of GHG emissions in our team’s home state. Electric cars offer drastic reductions in GHG emissions over those powered by internal combustion engines, and are relatively easy to implement. With CityPlug’s simple metrics and AI insights, it is easier to build the robust, efficient charging network needed to facilitate the transition to EVs. For example, California is estimated to need 1.5 million more chargers by 2030, and more still to meet the state’s ambitious yet necessary goal of phasing out ICE car sales by 2035. Our project would enable this network to both be completed faster, as it would not only shorten the time to identify candidate sites, but would also provide for a more efficient network, with each charging location being selected by our algorithms to serve its demand most effectively. In facilitating this transition, we would be able to not only help abate climate change and its associated natural disasters, but would also help to tackle its other effects, including death and sickness from pollution, specifically vehicle emissions which have historically hit lower-income areas hardest. Pollution is considered in our model, which helps us identify areas in which the introduction of EV’s would create the most profound benefit, by way of relief from deadly pollutants. Due to the unique factors our project considers, the result of meetings with industry professionals and EV users conducted throughout the course of our development, and the unique nature of our solution, we would seek a patent to protect our innovation, specifically, our two models and the values which they use in their analysis. Such protection is necessary due to the novel approach of our solution, using two algorithms and clusters with regional insights, as opposed to the manual approach taken by many present industry leaders. We have looked into the patenting process, and have researched and made preliminary plans for a patent, to be used once the development of our innovation is finalized enough to support it.
